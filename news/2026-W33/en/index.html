Claude Raises Riemann Zeta Zeros to 67.2%: Two Papers No One Had Combined

What happened?

Anthropic revealed that its unreleased research Claude model managed to raise the proven fraction of Riemann zeta zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%. It's the biggest single-step jump in the history of this problem, achieved by combining two math papers that no one had combined before, using 60 AI sub-agents working in parallel.

Why it matters

This matters because the Riemann hypothesis is one of the most famous open problems in mathematics, with a one-million-dollar prize. An AI advancing it overnight doesn't change your daily life, but it shows that AI models can already make real mathematical discoveries, speed up scientific research, and find connections between papers that humans overlook. For mathematicians and scientists, it's a powerful new tool; for everyone else, it's a sign that AI is moving beyond being a fancy calculator and becoming a partner in pure reasoning.

Impact on your daily life

The Riemann hypothesis, formulated by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, states that all nontrivial zeros of the zeta function have real part equal to 1/2, the so-called critical line. For over 160 years, nobody has been able to prove or disprove it, which is why it's one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Problems. Before this advance, the best known result had only proven that 41.6% of those zeros lie on the critical line; Anthropic's research model combined two existing approaches that had never been put together and pushed the mark to 67.2%.
