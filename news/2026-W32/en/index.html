anthropic.com

It's official: Anthropic is building an in-house chip team for Claude

What happened?

Anthropic has posted a job listing and confirmed it is building an internal team of engineers to design custom chips for its Claude model. The company would stop relying solely on external suppliers like Nvidia and create its own tailored hardware.

Why it matters

This matters because training and running AI models is extremely expensive. If Anthropic makes its own chips, it can cut costs, improve efficiency, and offer a faster and cheaper Claude. For users, it could mean better prices, speed, and AI capabilities. It also pressures Nvidia to keep innovating.

Impact on your daily life

Until now, Anthropic used chips from Nvidia and Google (TPUs). Designing its own chip is a strategic move to control the whole process, similar to what giants like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft already do. It's a signal that Anthropic wants to compete in the AI race not just with software, but also with proprietary hardware, reducing its vulnerability to chip shortages and high prices.

Sources: anthropic.com · businessinsider.com

gob.mx

AI regulation in the workplace enters legislative agenda, new labor reforms promoted

What happened?

In Mexico, the debate on how to regulate artificial intelligence in the workplace has reached Congress. Reform initiatives were introduced that aim to protect jobs, require companies to disclose how algorithms that make decisions about workers (such as hiring or firing) work, and create stricter oversight mechanisms.

Why it matters

This matters because algorithms in the workplace are not science fiction: many companies already use them for recruiting, evaluating, or even deciding on layoffs. If these reforms advance, workers would have the right to know why a system rejects or rewards them, and there would be institutions to turn to if they believe the machine got it wrong. For everyday people, it means their employment could be better protected against automated decisions that are currently opaque.

Impact on your daily life

The issue is not new: for years, international organizations and other countries have discussed the risks of algorithmic bias. In Mexico, the previous administration had released an AI strategy, but it didn't touch the labor sphere. Now, with the rise of generative tools and automation, unions and some lawmakers have pushed for the law to set limits before it's too late.

Sources: gob.mx · diputados.gob.mx · eleconomista.com.mx

Google Confirms Gemini 4 Will Replace the Gemini 3.5 Pro

What happened?

Google DeepMind has confirmed that Gemini 4 will be the next big model and will replace Gemini 3.5 Pro, which was left behind after several delays. The company has stopped talking about a release date for Gemini 3.5 Pro and is now putting all its development muscle behind the fourth generation.

Why it matters

If you were waiting for Gemini 3.5 Pro to improve your chats or connect it to your work tools, the plan just changed: that upgrade is no longer coming. Developers and product teams that built around that version will have to shift their bets to Gemini 4, and everyday users will get the new features in one bigger release instead of a smaller intermediate update.

Impact on your daily life

This is not the first time Google has changed its roadmap halfway through. Gemini 3.5 Pro had been suffering from delays and internal tension at DeepMind while competitors kept shipping models. With this announcement, Google is trying to look decisive and concentrate resources on a more ambitious bet, even though everything promised for 3.5 Pro is now up in the air.

Sources: deepmind.google · blog.google · www.geeky-gadgets.com

Google expands Gemini lineup with cheaper models and new Mythos rival

What happened?

Google updated its Gemini lineup with cheaper, more efficient models, especially Gemini Flash, and also introduced a new cybersecurity offering that goes head-to-head with Mythos. The goal is to fill gaps it still had against other rivals and put pressure on price.

Why it matters

For everyday users, this can mean paying less for AI in daily tasks, from drafting emails to generating images, and getting faster responses. The cybersecurity side is aimed at businesses, but if those tools protect services you use every day, the benefit reaches you indirectly as well.

Impact on your daily life

Google has spent years growing Gemini to compete with OpenAI and Anthropic, but it lacked a clear option in AI-powered security. Mythos had made a name for itself in that niche, so this move is about staying relevant in a market where cost and specialized features matter more and more.
