DeepSeek invests $20.8 million in Unitree's Shanghai IPO

What happened?

DeepSeek put $20.8 million (140.8 million yuan) into Unitree's initial public offering on the Shanghai stock exchange. According to the exchange filing, the two companies also agreed to jointly develop AI models designed for humanoid robots.

Why it matters

This investment is not just about money: it brings one of the world's most aggressive AI startups together with a leading robot maker. For everyday people, it could mean that in a few years we see humanoid robots with genuinely smart brains capable of helping at home or in factories, but it also raises questions about which jobs they might replace. It's a clear sign that the race for advanced AI robotics is speeding up and big players want to be at the center.

Impact on your daily life

DeepSeek became famous for creating powerful language models with much lower training costs than its competitors, which shook the market. Unitree already has robots like the H1 and G1 that walk, run, and do acrobatics. This alliance aims to fuse the brain (DeepSeek's conversational and reasoning AI) with the body (Unitree's robots). In 2026, humanoid robots are booming: Tesla, Figure, and other companies are also fighting for that niche, but the combination of two Chinese firms could speed up development and lower costs.

Claude Vs ChatGPT: How AI Assistants Differ

What happened?

Engadget published a side-by-side comparison of Claude, Anthropic's assistant, and ChatGPT, OpenAI's assistant, across everyday tasks. The main finding is that ChatGPT lags behind in several areas and hallucinates far more often than Claude.

Why it matters

Hallucinations matter because ChatGPT can state false facts with total confidence, so any task that relies on real data needs extra fact-checking. For people using AI to draft documents, study, or write code, that means Claude demands less supervision and makes the tool more reliable in high-stakes work.

Impact on your daily life

Anthropic and OpenAI have long competed on opposite strategies: OpenAI focuses on reach and integrations, while Anthropic markets safety and cautious answers. This comparison lands as teams choose which assistant to embed in their workflows, and a higher hallucination rate can tip enterprise deals.

apple.com

Apple says Mac users in China can connect to Alibaba's Qwen AI service

What happened?

Apple published an official guide allowing eligible Mac users in mainland China to connect Alibaba's Qwen AI service to the Siri assistant and the Writing Tools feature. The guide walks through setup steps and which Mac models are supported.

Why it matters

For Mac owners in China, this means they can finally use AI in everyday tasks without tricky workarounds: ask Siri to summarize emails, draft replies, or polish text in Writing Tools, all powered by Alibaba's Qwen model and compliant with local rules. Companies using Macs also benefit because their teams get AI tools without breaking Chinese regulations, and Apple stays competitive in a market where local AI is a must.

Impact on your daily life

China requires generative AI services to be offered by licensed local companies, which is why models like OpenAI's ChatGPT or Google DeepMind's Gemini are unavailable there. Apple had already partnered with Alibaba to bring Qwen to iPhones in China, and now it is expanding that integration to Macs. Alibaba is a major Chinese tech giant with a strong AI portfolio, and its Qwen models are widely adopted in the Chinese business ecosystem.

Sources: apple.com · alibabacloud.com · reuters.com
